#### Description

`Salesforce/blip-image-captioning-base` is the Transformers-format release of the BLIP *base* model fine-tuned for image captioning, from "BLIP: Bootstrapping Language-Image Pre-training for Unified Vision-Language Understanding and Generation" (Li, Li, Xiong, Hoi, arXiv:2201.12086), converted by the Hugging Face team and pinned here to revision `82a37760796d32b1411fe092ab5d4e227313294b` (the Hub's `main` on 2026-09-14). The snapshot `config.json` declares `BlipForConditionalGeneration`: a 12-layer ViT-B/16 image encoder at 384×384 (hidden size 768, 12 heads, 576 patches plus a class token) and a 12-layer BERT-style text decoder (hidden size 768, vocabulary 30,524, maximum 512 positions, `max_length` 20) whose layers cross-attend to the image features and generate the caption — 223,971,644 parameters in the 990 MB float32 `pytorch_model.bin` (the Hub's *247M* counts the decoder's tied output projection twice). The model was pretrained on 129M image–text pairs (COCO, Visual Genome, Conceptual Captions 3M/12M, SBU and a LAION subset, bootstrapped with BLIP's captioning-and-filtering procedure) and fine-tuned on COCO Captions. At inference the processor (`BlipImageProcessor`, `preprocessor_config.json`: resize to 384×384 without preserving aspect ratio, CLIP mean/std) encodes the image and the decoder generates a sentence from the BOS token (unconditional) or continues a tokenised prefix (conditional, the upstream README's `a photography of` example). The pipeline also carries a bounded adaptation contract: `adapt` fine-tunes the last *k* blocks of the caption decoder plus its head transform and bias (two blocks by default, 19,526,204 parameters; the vision encoder, every embedding and the tied output projection stay frozen) on a validated `{id, image, captions}` dataset, training on every (image, reference caption) pair with the frozen vision features computed once, selecting the epoch by validation CIDEr-D, and `save_artifact` / `from_artifact` export and reload exactly those tensors as safetensors with a manifest bound to this base revision and weight digest. The tutorial's default corpus is VizWiz-Captions (`mm-eval/VizWiz-Captions` on the Hub at `c4a6d897836e7885d0095134f92d392e4e770539`, CC BY 4.0): the four text columns of one pinned shard read column-only over HTTPS range requests and the 336 photographs of its first row group read the same way, each pinned by size and SHA-256. **Weight format:** upstream hosts no SafeTensors at this revision — only `pytorch_model.bin` (a pickle) and `tf_model.h5` (the TensorFlow port). This package executes the pickle after its manifest SHA-256 is checked, with `weights_only=True`; DIMER does not accept `.bin` uploads, so the profile's hosted blob is `tf_model.h5`, a different file this package never loads (both digests under §Immutable provenance). What this repository adds is packaging: `verify_snapshot` and `stage_missing_files` (manifest digest checking and fresh-clone staging), `BlipCaptioningPipeline.from_pretrained` (verified local loading with `trust_remote_code=False`, `use_safetensors=False`, `weights_only=True`), `caption` (input validation, an optional whitespace-collapsed prefix, greedy single-beam decoding under a caller-owned `max_new_tokens`, a `truncated` flag), `normalize_caption`, `caption_tokens`, `keyword_hits`, `unigram_f1`, the `validate_inputs` and `evaluation_report` stage helpers, the corpus metrics `bleu4`, `rouge_l`, `cider_d` and `caption_metrics` with the `constant_caption_baseline` and `colour_neighbour_baseline` (pure Python, in `metrics.py`), the dataset contract `validate_dataset`, `split_dataset`, `check_split_disjoint`, `load_byod_dataset`, `write_dataset_jsonl` and the pinned-corpus readers `fetch_annotations`, `fetch_images`, `build_sample_dataset`, `fetch_sample_dataset` (in `samples.py`), and the adaptation contract `evaluate`, `adapt`, `save_artifact`, `load_artifact`, `from_artifact`.

#### Intended Use and Limitations

The uses below are the ones the package was built to support; everything else is either out of scope (§Out-of-scope use cases) or prohibited (§Use cases).

###### Primary Intended Uses

The task is single-sentence image captioning: input one image (`PIL.Image.Image`, any mode, converted to RGB), an optional prefix (up to 128 characters) and a token budget; output one caption string, the number of tokens generated and whether the budget was exhausted. Envisioned applications are draft alt-text and image descriptions for later human editing, keyword-free indexing and search over photo collections, first-pass content summaries in media tooling, and prototyping of multimodal assistants — with the caption checked against the image by a human before it is shown to anyone who cannot see the image. The second intended use is bounded supervised adaptation: given a small labelled sample of the deployment's own photographs with one or more human reference captions each, `adapt` fine-tunes the caption decoder's last blocks and head and `evaluate` reports BLEU-4, ROUGE-L, CIDEr-D and unigram F1 on an image-disjoint held-out split beside two non-neural baselines, so an operator can measure whether the base model's COCO-style captions move toward their corpus's vocabulary and style before deciding anything. Within DIMER the pipeline is an inference component, a zero-configuration baseline for captioning and a bounded adaptation contract, not a certified describer for any specific image domain.

###### Primary Intended Users

Intended users are machine-learning engineers, computer-vision developers, and content or accessibility tooling developers integrating image captioning into research prototypes, internal tooling, or the DIMER workbench, and adapting it to an image population for which they hold a small set of photographs with reference captions. A user is expected to understand that the output is *generated text* — it carries no probability, no correctness signal and no location in the image, and the model captions every image, including blank or meaningless ones, with equal fluency — that captions follow COCO's style (one short English sentence naming salient objects, often with hallucinated attributes such as colours or backgrounds), that a prefix makes the caption partly the caller's sentence, that the token budget is theirs to set, that the model was fine-tuned on photographs so drawings, documents, diagrams and expert imagery are distribution shifts, that greedy decoding is reproducible on a fixed device but GPU and CPU outputs need not match and differ from the upstream beam-search evaluation, and that quality can only be measured on reference captions they supply. A user of `adapt` is expected to understand that the frozen model is already a competent captioner, that a few hundred photographs move the consensus metric by hundredths while BLEU-4 can move the other way as sentences lengthen, that one seeded split gives no dispersion estimate, and that an adapter is a derived work of the weights and of the reference captions it trained on. Users who need text reading, question answering, region captions or batch throughput are expected to know none of that is provided here.

###### Out-of-scope use cases

1. **Capability boundary:** no localisation or evidence region, no confidence, no abstention (the model cannot say "nothing to describe"), no text reading (BLIP is not an OCR model), no visual question answering (a separate checkpoint), no dense or multi-sentence description, no batching (each image is a full encoder pass), no sampling, beam search or repetition control (greedy, one beam), and no guarantee of counting, colour or spatial accuracy — the smoke run called an orange a second apple and described a blank image as a striped rug. Not a general training framework: `adapt` fine-tunes only whole blocks of the caption decoder plus its head transform and bias (1..12 blocks, no LoRA, no vision-encoder or embedding training, no scheduler, epochs capped at 20, learning rate at 1e-3, batch at 64), selects an epoch on validation CIDEr-D, and writes one artifact format; SPICE and METEOR are not implemented, CIDEr-D's document frequencies come from the evaluated set rather than a fixed reference corpus, so its absolute value is not comparable to leaderboard numbers.
2. **Input boundary:** `caption` rejects non-PIL images (`TypeError`), sides below `MIN_IMAGE_SIDE = 16` px or above `MAX_IMAGE_SIDE = 4096` px, a prefix that is not a string or is empty or longer than `MAX_PREFIX_CHARS = 128`, and budgets outside `[1, MAX_NEW_TOKENS = 64]` (`ValueError`/`TypeError`). Every image is resized to 384×384 without preserving aspect ratio, so thin panoramas are squashed and detail below roughly 1/24 of the shorter side is lost; fine text, small objects and subtle colours are outside the model's resolution. Datasets for `validate_dataset`, `evaluate` and `adapt` are lists of `{id, image, captions}` records: fewer than `MIN_RECORDS = 8` or more than `MAX_RECORDS = 5000` records, a duplicate or malformed id, an image that does not exist or does not decode, an empty caption list, a caption over `MAX_CAPTION_CHARS = 500` or a prefix over `MAX_PREFIX_CHARS` are refused with `ValueError` before any model import; `split_dataset` refuses fractions that leave fewer than 8 training records; `load_artifact` refuses an adapter whose format, version, base id, revision, weight file or digest differ, whose weight path escapes the artifact directory, or whose tensor set differs from the recorded configuration.
3. **Input boundary:** the fine-tuning data is COCO Captions — everyday photographs with five crowd-sourced English captions each. Drawings, screenshots, documents, charts, medical or satellite imagery and artwork fall outside what the upstream authors evaluated and what this repository measured; results on them are undefined, not merely degraded (the tutorial's cartoons are exactly such inputs, which is why their captions are observations, not measurements). An image with no content still produces a caption (see §Risks and harms). The adaptation sample is VizWiz-Captions — photographs by blind users, often blurry or badly framed, captions that name what is held and what a label says — and the recorded gain is specific to that population and caption style.
4. **Decision boundary:** not for autonomous decisions that act on captions — publishing alt-text unreviewed to blind or low-vision users, content moderation, safety or compliance labelling, surveillance descriptions of people — without a human comparing the caption with the image, and a locally measured CIDEr/BLEU on the deployment's own reference-captioned images. A gain on the operator's validation split is not a release decision: it is a single seeded split of one corpus with no dispersion estimate, and an adapter that learned a corpus's phrasing can apply it to images the phrasing does not fit.

#### Factors

###### Groups

This pipeline is human-centric wherever the image contains people: COCO captions describe people by apparent gender, age, activity and dress ("a man", "a young girl", "an old woman"), and the model reproduces those conventions — including the annotators' guesses — for anyone it depicts, with no signal that it is guessing. Published audits of COCO document gender-imbalanced activity associations and a skew towards Western, adult, lighter-skinned subjects; neither the upstream authors nor this repository audited caption accuracy or caption *content* (which attributes are mentioned, which words are chosen) by the depicted person's gender, age, skin tone, disability, dress or setting, nor the pretraining web corpora for stereotyped image–text pairs. Non-human groups whose accuracy is unknown, not known to be equal: non-photographic images (the tutorial's cartoons; the model miscounted their fruit), non-Western scenes, objects, food and clothing, low-light and low-resolution captures, and images whose salient content is text. An operator whose images contain people is responsible for a fairness audit on their own image set, stratified by depicted group, before relying on the output — and for deciding whether descriptions of people are surfaced at all (see §Use cases).

###### Instrumentation

The upstream pretraining "instrument" is web image–text pairs at whatever resolution and quality the source sites served, filtered by BLIP's own captioner/filter, and the fine-tuning instrument is COCO photography (consumer cameras, Flickr uploads) with crowd-written captions. Inference images arrive from whatever produced them — a phone camera, a screenshot, a render, a scanner — and resolution, exposure, colour balance, compression and aspect ratio all change the visual evidence; the fixed 384×384 resize discards aspect ratio and detail regardless of the source, so a 4096×4096 input carries no more information than a 384×384 one. The pipeline validates type, size and prefix length only; it cannot detect an unusual capture or a non-photographic image. The synthetic tutorial scenes (flat Pillow shapes, no texture, no lighting) are themselves a rendering instrument unlike any COCO photograph, which is why the smoke run's miscount is recorded rather than treated as an anomaly.

###### Environment

Operating environment: Python 3.12 with `torch==2.14.0`, `torchvision==0.29.0`, `torchaudio==2.11.0`, `transformers==4.57.6`, `safetensors==0.8.0` (used only for the adapter artifact; the base checkpoint is a pickle), `numpy==2.5.3`, `pillow==11.3.0`, `huggingface-hub==0.36.2`, `pyarrow==25.0.1`, float32 on CPU; CUDA is used automatically when visible (float32). Inference, measured on the reference machine with the GPU hidden (`CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=-1`) and the Hub offline (`HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1`): `verify_snapshot` on the 8-file, 990 MB snapshot (included in the load); load 3.9 s with exactly two `torch.load(weights_only=True)` calls observed; three drawn scenes, unconditional and with two prefixes, 0.18–0.54 s per caption (2–11 tokens; first call slower); a 4096×4096 blank image 0.49 s — cost is one 576-patch encoder pass plus a few decoder steps per image, roughly independent of the input resolution after the resize. Adaptation, measured on the RTX 5070 Ti (WSL, `cuda:0`, float32) in the build record: the 208 training photographs' vision features are computed once (about 5 s), each epoch of about 940 (image, caption) pairs at batch 8 plus the 40-photograph validation scoring takes about 25 s, and the four-epoch default recipe about 117 s including the frozen validation pass; the CPU pre-flight of the same recipe is recorded in `docs/release-verification.md`. Data environment: the model assumes the image is an everyday photograph and, when a prefix is given, that the prefix is the start of a COCO-style sentence; the synthetic tutorial scenes violate the first assumption on purpose (flat cartoons), which is where the recorded miscount comes from. Documents, diagrams, artwork, empty or corrupted images violate it to degrees this repository did not measure, and the pipeline reports no signal when they do.

#### Metrics

###### Performance Measures

Four performance measures, named exactly as the code names them and implemented in pure Python in `metrics.py` after the `coco-caption` definitions: `bleu4` — corpus-level BLEU-4, clipped 1..4-gram precision against all references with the closest-length brevity penalty; `rouge_l` — the longest-common-subsequence F-measure (beta 1.2) against the best-matching reference, averaged over images; `cider_d` — TF-IDF-weighted 1..4-gram cosine consensus with the references, Gaussian length penalty (sigma 6), clipped counts, scaled by 10, with document frequencies computed over the references of the evaluated set (so scores compare systems on the same records and are not leaderboard values); and `unigram_f1`. All apply `normalize_caption` (lower-cased, punctuation removed, whitespace collapsed). `caption_metrics` averages them over a record set and adds `empty_rate` and `mean_words`; `evaluate` runs the model over a validated dataset and returns them with the verdict `measured` (at least `MIN_SCORED_RECORDS = 50` records) or `measured-small-sample`. Two non-neural baselines frame every corpus number: `constant_caption_baseline` (the training caption with the highest mean CIDEr-D against all other training references, for every image) and `colour_neighbour_baseline` (the first reference of the training photograph whose 3×3 mean-colour grid is closest). The build record on the VizWiz sample (test split of 70; CPU pre-flight, with the RTX 5070 Ti run in parentheses where it differs): constant caption CIDEr-D 0.052, colour neighbour 0.042, frozen model 0.590 (BLEU-4 0.213, ROUGE-L 0.453; `no-text` 0.525, `text` 0.638), adapted 0.629 (0.627) (BLEU-4 0.190, ROUGE-L 0.439, mean length 8.9 → 11.2 words; `no-text` 0.615, `text` 0.639) — a small gain on the consensus metric, carried by the photographs without text, with the n-gram-precision metrics slightly down, one seeded split, no dispersion. The caption itself is generated text with no score, probability or correctness signal; `new_tokens` and `truncated` describe the generation, not its quality. METEOR and SPICE are not implemented and COCO Captions is not bundled. The repository also ships two lighter helpers because they are what a caller can compute on their own labelled sample: `unigram_f1(prediction, references)` — bag-of-words F1 after normalisation (lower-cased, punctuation removed, whitespace collapsed) against the best-matching reference, multiset overlap — and `keyword_hits(caption, keywords)`, whole-token presence of caller-chosen keywords. Both are plumbing checks, stated as such. The public `evaluation_report(results, references=None)` stage returns the report in machine-readable form: the mean `unigram_f1` plus one per-image entry (prediction, references, score) with the verdict `sample-sanity` when references are supplied, or the verdict `not-measurable` naming the labelled set that would be required when they are not — which is the tutorial's default path, by design. The upstream paper's COCO Captions CIDEr (about 130 for its ViT-B models with beam search, upstream-reported; which pretraining variant the hosted checkpoint corresponds to is not stated on the upstream card) is not reproduced or claimed by this pipeline.

###### Decision thresholds

No score threshold exists in the model: it generates tokens until end-of-sequence or the budget and nothing is filtered or abstained. The decision parameters are the **token budget** `max_new_tokens`, default `DEFAULT_MAX_NEW_TOKENS = 30` (a sentence-sized budget chosen by this repository; the checkpoint's own `text_config.max_length` is 20 and the smoke captions needed 2–11 tokens including end-of-sequence) with ceiling `MAX_NEW_TOKENS = 64`, and the optional **prefix**, which the model continues verbatim and which therefore shapes the caption (`a drawing of` yielded `a drawing of a house and tree`; no prefix yielded `a red house with a tree and a ball`). A budget that is too small is reported, not hidden: `truncated` is true whenever `new_tokens` reaches it, and the caller should raise the budget and rerun. Decoding is greedy (`do_sample=False`, `num_beams=1`) with no temperature, beams or repetition penalty — a reproducibility choice that departs from the upstream beam-search evaluation setting. A deployment owns choosing the budget and prefix and deciding how a caption is verified against the image before it is used.

###### Approaches to uncertainty and variability

Every reported corpus metric is one value on one seeded split of one corpus: the 336 photographs are the first row group of the pinned shard (not a random draw), `build_sample_dataset(seed=42)` fixes the assignment of the 318 captioned ones into 208 / 40 / 70, `adapt(seed=0)` fixes the shuffle order, and no repetition over seeds or splits is performed, so no confidence interval or standard deviation is available and none is claimed — the gain on the test split is a point estimate whose spread is unknown, the validation split that picks the epoch holds 40 photographs, and the build record's own sweep shows the estimate's fragility: 5e-5 on the same data overfit within two epochs (validation CIDEr-D 0.675 → 0.528 by epoch 4, selector kept epoch 0), 2e-5 over twice the photographs lowered held-out CIDEr-D to 0.496, and 1e-5 for six epochs peaked at epoch 4 and declined after. Inference run-to-run variability comes only from floating-point kernel selection across CPU builds and accelerators; there is no sampling and no seed to set, so a fixed input on fixed hardware is repeatable, but because decoding is autoregressive a single differing token changes the rest of the sentence, and CPU and CUDA outputs need not match; the drawn scenes use no text rendering, so their bytes do not depend on the Pillow build. On the drawn scenes the model produced `a red house with a tree and a ball`, `a beach scene with a sail and a sun` and `a red and yellow apple on a white background` (the orange became an apple) — three observations on flat cartoons, not an estimate; on a blank white image it produced `a white and black striped rug with a white border` and on uniform noise `a very colorful and very colorful tv screen`, which is what an uncalibrated generator with no abstention looks like. A caller who needs a quality estimate must supply several reference captions per image and compute CIDEr/BLEU over a corpus or bootstrap resamples themselves; a caller who needs a confidence per caption has none from this model.

#### Ethical considerations and biases

No external ethics board, red-team, or population-specific clearance reviewed this repository or, to our knowledge, the upstream checkpoint; nothing below should be read as implying one.

###### Data

The upstream paper describes pretraining on 129M image–text pairs from COCO, Visual Genome, Conceptual Captions 3M and 12M, SBU Captions and a 115M-image LAION subset — web-scraped pairs whose images and captions can include real, identifiable people, copyrighted photographs and stereotyped or offensive text, filtered only by BLIP's own learned filter — and fine-tuning on COCO Captions, whose images are Flickr photographs of people and everyday scenes with crowd-written descriptions; personal data in the training corpora is therefore present by construction. Neither was audited here. The tutorial's adaptation corpus is VizWiz-Captions (Gurari et al., ECCV 2020; CC BY 4.0), photographs taken by blind people of their own surroundings — homes, documents, medication, food, people — with crowd-written captions; the VizWiz authors describe removing images with private content, but the photographs still show personal spaces and belongings, and the captions transcribe labels and screens. This repository reads the captions of one pinned shard column-only and 336 photographs from one row group at run time and does not redistribute either. This repository distributes code, tests, and documentation; it does not distribute the 989,820,849-byte `pytorch_model.bin` (staged locally under `weights/blip-image-captioning-base/` and git-ignored) nor the 990,275,136-byte `tf_model.h5` (never fetched by this package), and it ships no sample photographs — the tutorial scenes are drawn in code and the VizWiz photographs are fetched and digest-verified at run time into a git-ignored cache. An adapter written by `save_artifact` carries no training images, but its tensors were shaped by the reference captions, and the operator is responsible for the rights attached to those. The operator must audit the images they submit for personal, proprietary, or otherwise restricted content; the pipeline performs no such check and will describe a person's appearance as readily as a landscape.

###### Human Life

This pipeline is not intended for decisions in health, safety, criminal justice, employment, credit, or housing, and it has not been validated or certified for any of them by this repository, the upstream authors, or any regulator. Its only validation is the offline unit suite (36 tests, plus 6 model-backed tests that run when the snapshot is staged, one of them on CUDA) and the CPU and GPU runs recorded in this repository on three drawn scenes and one VizWiz sample (the adaptation lifted the `no-text` photographs from 0.525 to 0.615 CIDEr-D and left the `text` ones at 0.64 — it learned the corpus's style, not to read). Foreseeable but unintended sensitive uses — publishing captions as alt-text to blind or low-vision users where a wrong description misleads, describing people in surveillance or bodycam footage, labelling user-uploaded images for moderation or safety, describing medical or hazard imagery — would be admissible only with human review of every caption against the image (the model invents content when there is none and gives no signal), a locally measured reference-based score on the deployment's own captioned images stratified by depicted group, a documented budget and prefix policy with `truncated` handling, an explicit policy on whether people are described at all, and whatever regulatory clearance the domain requires.

###### Mitigations

- **Supply-chain integrity:** `MODEL_REVISION` is a 40-hex commit; `stage_missing_files` refuses a manifest whose `modelId`/`revision` differ from the package constants and fetches only manifest-listed files at that revision when `allow_download=True`; `verify_snapshot` then checks all 8 listed files' byte sizes and SHA-256 before any load — **including the pickle checkpoint, so a modified `pytorch_model.bin` never reaches a deserialiser**; `from_pretrained` loads only from the verified directory with `local_files_only=True`, always passes `trust_remote_code=False`, `use_safetensors=False` (naming the format) and `weights_only=True` (restricted unpickler: tensors, primitives and containers only), and the smoke run loaded and captioned with `HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1` while observing exactly two `torch.load(weights_only=True)` calls. The upstream `tf_model.h5` is neither listed nor loaded. A test flips one hex digit of a manifest digest and asserts the loader refuses; another asserts a foreign manifest is refused; the import-boundary tests assert that a missing or tampered snapshot is refused before `torch` or `transformers` is imported; another asserts the manifest lists the pickle and not the TensorFlow file.
- **Input integrity:** the public `validate_inputs(images, *, prefix, max_new_tokens)` stage applies exactly the checks `caption` applies (both route through one shared private checker) and returns an input manifest recording the schema, the ceilings, the observed inputs, the checked prefix, the budget and the verdict; `validate_image` rejects non-PIL inputs and sides outside 16–4096 px; non-string, empty or over-long prefixes and out-of-range or boolean budgets are rejected; `caption` raises on a malformed runner result; `evaluation_report` rejects mismatched or empty reference lists; `unigram_f1` rejects an empty reference set.
- **Reproducibility:** exact `==` pins in `pyproject.toml`; greedy single-beam decoding with no sampling; every result carries `model_id`, `model_revision`, the checked prefix, the budget, `new_tokens`, `truncated`, the device and the dtype.
- **Refusals:** no batching, no download without the explicit flag, no Hub access at inference time, no sampling, no unrestricted pickle deserialisation, no attempt to guess whether the image has anything to describe, and no filtering of caption content — that policy is the operator's to implement around the pipeline.
- **Adaptation integrity:** `adapt` validates the dataset before any tensor is built, trains only the named tensors with every other parameter's `requires_grad` false, restores the initial tensors and re-freezes the model if anything raises (including the caller's progress callback), and keeps the epoch with the best validation CIDEr-D rather than the last; `save_artifact` records the base id, revision, weight digest, tensor names, file size and SHA-256; `load_artifact` verifies format, version, base, path containment, digest and the exact tensor set before `safetensors` deserialises anything and refuses any tensor outside the caption decoder. Split leakage is prevented by construction (`split_dataset` keeps every record on an image in one split; `check_split_disjoint` asserts it); baselines are computed on the same records as the model.
- No statistical mitigation (class balancing, subsampling) applies: the adaptation samples pairs uniformly and the reported metrics are unweighted means; a corpus with one reference per image trains the model on one annotator's phrasing.

###### Risks and harms

- **Invented content:** the model has no abstention — a blank image yielded a striped rug and noise a colourful TV screen in the smoke run — so an empty, corrupted or unusual image produces a fluent, plausible caption with no signal; downstream consumers that trust the string (alt-text pipelines, search indexes, LLM pipelines) inherit the error silently.
- **Hallucinated attributes:** colours, backgrounds, counts and objects that are not there (`yellow apple`, `white background`, a second apple) are stated with the same fluency as correct ones; only reference-based evaluation on the deployment's images shows the rate, and the tutorial measures none.
- **Descriptions of people:** apparent gender, age and activity are asserted from appearance in COCO's conventions; misgendering, age errors and stereotyped activity associations are foreseeable and can cause direct harm when surfaced.
- **Prefix steering:** a conditional caption is partly the caller's sentence; a leading prefix (`a photo of a criminal`) makes the model complete it.
- **Automation bias:** a grammatical one-sentence caption invites trust that generated text has not earned.
- **Truncation:** a caption longer than the budget is cut (reported via `truncated`); a caller who ignores the flag ships a partial sentence.
- **Pickle checkpoint:** the executed weight file is a pickle; the digest check and `weights_only=True` are the mitigation, and a caller who bypasses `from_pretrained` and loads the file another way forfeits both.
- **Bias amplification:** any image population COCO under-represents (non-Western scenes, non-photographic images, minority groups) is reproduced as uneven caption quality and word choice, undetected because no per-group evaluation exists.
- **Style capture, not truth:** an adapter trained on a corpus of captions that transcribe labels (`a bottle of Tylenol`) learns to produce that shape of sentence, and will produce a label the image does not show as fluently as one it does — CIDEr-D rewards the vocabulary, not its truth.
- **Overfitting on small data:** a few hundred photographs at too high a learning rate degrade the base model within two epochs (build record); the validation-based selector reports it by keeping epoch 0, but only if a validation split is supplied.
- **Resource use:** a 990 MB model and ~0.2–0.5 s per image on the reference CPU; an image-heavy workload scales linearly. Adaptation holds the vision features of every training photograph in memory (576×768 floats each) and the optimiser state of 19.5 M parameters; the default recipe takes about two minutes on the RTX 5070 Ti and 487 s on the reference CPU.

###### Use cases

Prohibited even where the model would work: captioning images of people in order to infer or record protected characteristics (race, ethnicity, religion, health, disability, sexual orientation), to identify, track, profile or surveil individuals, or to make or support decisions in employment, housing, credit, insurance, education, healthcare access, law enforcement or immigration; processing images the operator has no right to process, including intimate imagery and licence-restricted material; fine-tuning on photographs or captions whose subjects or authors have not consented to that processing where consent is required; publishing captions as verified descriptions, evidence or alt-text without human review; steering the model with prefixes to produce defamatory, sexual or demeaning descriptions of real people; and any use that violates the upstream BSD-3-Clause licence terms (including use of the Salesforce name for endorsement), the DIMER deployment terms, or the consent and data-protection obligations attached to the images processed. Autonomous high-consequence actions triggered by unreviewed captions are prohibited by the intended-use contract above.

## Runtime

- Pins: `torch==2.14.0`, `torchvision==0.29.0`, `torchaudio==2.11.0`, `transformers==4.57.6`, `safetensors==0.8.0`, `numpy==2.5.3`, `pillow==11.3.0`, `huggingface-hub==0.36.2`, `pyarrow==25.0.1`; Python 3.12.
- Precision: float32; preprocessing resizes the image to 384×384 (aspect ratio not preserved) and normalises with CLIP mean/std (`BlipImageProcessor`, snapshot defaults); an optional prefix is tokenised by the snapshot's BERT tokenizer; greedy single-beam decoding.
- Measured 2026-09-14 in the Windows venv (`torch 2.14.0+cu130`) with `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=-1` and `HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1`, device `cpu`: load 3.9 s (verify included; two `torch.load(weights_only=True)` calls on `pytorch_model.bin` observed); `caption` on three synthetic cartoon scenes drawn with Pillow — house scene (640×480): no prefix `a red house with a tree and a ball` (10 tokens, 0.54 s first call), `a photography of` → `a photography of a red house in a green field` (8 tokens, 0.26 s), `a drawing of` → `a drawing of a house and tree` (0.22 s); beach scene (640×480): `a beach scene with a sail and a sun`, `a photography of a beach scene`, `a drawing of a beach with a sail and a sun`; fruit scene (480×480, an apple and an orange): `a red and yellow apple on a white background`, `a photography of two apples`, `a drawing of two apples on a white background`; 0.18–0.28 s each after the first; blank 384×384 and 4096×4096 white images → `a white and black striped rug with a white border` (11 tokens; 0.49 s at 4096²); uniform noise → `a very colorful and very colorful tv screen`; `max_new_tokens=3` → `a red house`, `truncated` true; `keyword_hits` on the house caption: house/tree/red present, sun absent.
