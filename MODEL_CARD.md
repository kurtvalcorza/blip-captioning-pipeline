#### Description

`Salesforce/blip-image-captioning-base` is the Transformers-format release of the BLIP *base* model fine-tuned for image captioning, from "BLIP: Bootstrapping Language-Image Pre-training for Unified Vision-Language Understanding and Generation" (Li, Li, Xiong, Hoi, arXiv:2201.12086), converted by the Hugging Face team and pinned here to revision `82a37760796d32b1411fe092ab5d4e227313294b` (the Hub's `main` on 2026-09-14). The snapshot `config.json` declares `BlipForConditionalGeneration`: a 12-layer ViT-B/16 image encoder at 384×384 (hidden size 768, 12 heads, 576 patches plus a class token) and a 12-layer BERT-style text decoder (hidden size 768, vocabulary 30,524, maximum 512 positions, `max_length` 20) whose layers cross-attend to the image features and generate the caption — about 247M parameters in the 990 MB float32 `pytorch_model.bin`. The model was pretrained on 129M image–text pairs (COCO, Visual Genome, Conceptual Captions 3M/12M, SBU and a LAION subset, bootstrapped with BLIP's captioning-and-filtering procedure) and fine-tuned on COCO Captions. At inference the processor (`BlipImageProcessor`, `preprocessor_config.json`: resize to 384×384 without preserving aspect ratio, CLIP mean/std) encodes the image and the decoder generates a sentence from the BOS token (unconditional) or continues a tokenised prefix (conditional, the upstream README's `a photography of` example). Nothing is trained or adapted here. **Weight format:** upstream hosts no SafeTensors at this revision — only `pytorch_model.bin` (a pickle) and `tf_model.h5` (the TensorFlow port). This package executes the pickle after its manifest SHA-256 is checked, with `weights_only=True`; DIMER does not accept `.bin` uploads, so the profile's hosted blob is `tf_model.h5`, a different file this package never loads (both digests under §Immutable provenance). What this repository adds is packaging: `verify_snapshot` and `stage_missing_files` (manifest digest checking and fresh-clone staging), `BlipCaptioningPipeline.from_pretrained` (verified local loading with `trust_remote_code=False`, `use_safetensors=False`, `weights_only=True`), `caption` (input validation, an optional whitespace-collapsed prefix, greedy single-beam decoding under a caller-owned `max_new_tokens`, a `truncated` flag), `normalize_caption`, `caption_tokens`, `keyword_hits`, `unigram_f1`, and the `validate_inputs` and `evaluation_report` stage helpers.

#### Intended Use and Limitations

The uses below are the ones the package was built to support; everything else is either out of scope (§Out-of-scope use cases) or prohibited (§Use cases).

###### Primary Intended Uses

The task is single-sentence image captioning: input one image (`PIL.Image.Image`, any mode, converted to RGB), an optional prefix (up to 128 characters) and a token budget; output one caption string, the number of tokens generated and whether the budget was exhausted. Envisioned applications are draft alt-text and image descriptions for later human editing, keyword-free indexing and search over photo collections, first-pass content summaries in media tooling, and prototyping of multimodal assistants — with the caption checked against the image by a human before it is shown to anyone who cannot see the image. Within DIMER the pipeline is an inference component and a zero-configuration baseline for captioning, not a certified describer for any specific image domain.

###### Primary Intended Users

Intended users are machine-learning engineers, computer-vision developers, and content or accessibility tooling developers integrating image captioning into research prototypes, internal tooling, or the DIMER workbench. A user is expected to understand that the output is *generated text* — it carries no probability, no correctness signal and no location in the image, and the model captions every image, including blank or meaningless ones, with equal fluency — that captions follow COCO's style (one short English sentence naming salient objects, often with hallucinated attributes such as colours or backgrounds), that a prefix makes the caption partly the caller's sentence, that the token budget is theirs to set, that the model was fine-tuned on photographs so drawings, documents, diagrams and expert imagery are distribution shifts, that greedy decoding is reproducible on a fixed device but GPU and CPU outputs need not match and differ from the upstream beam-search evaluation, and that quality can only be measured on reference captions they supply. Users who need text reading, question answering, region captions or batch throughput are expected to know none of that is provided here.

###### Out-of-scope use cases

1. **Capability boundary:** no localisation or evidence region, no confidence, no abstention (the model cannot say "nothing to describe"), no text reading (BLIP is not an OCR model), no visual question answering (a separate checkpoint), no dense or multi-sentence description, no batching (each image is a full encoder pass), no sampling, beam search or repetition control (greedy, one beam), and no guarantee of counting, colour or spatial accuracy — the smoke run called an orange a second apple and described a blank image as a striped rug.
2. **Input boundary:** `caption` rejects non-PIL images (`TypeError`), sides below `MIN_IMAGE_SIDE = 16` px or above `MAX_IMAGE_SIDE = 4096` px, a prefix that is not a string or is empty or longer than `MAX_PREFIX_CHARS = 128`, and budgets outside `[1, MAX_NEW_TOKENS = 64]` (`ValueError`/`TypeError`). Every image is resized to 384×384 without preserving aspect ratio, so thin panoramas are squashed and detail below roughly 1/24 of the shorter side is lost; fine text, small objects and subtle colours are outside the model's resolution.
3. **Input boundary:** the fine-tuning data is COCO Captions — everyday photographs with five crowd-sourced English captions each. Drawings, screenshots, documents, charts, medical or satellite imagery and artwork fall outside what the upstream authors evaluated and what this repository measured; results on them are undefined, not merely degraded (the tutorial's cartoons are exactly such inputs, which is why their captions are observations, not measurements). An image with no content still produces a caption (see §Risks and harms).
4. **Decision boundary:** not for autonomous decisions that act on captions — publishing alt-text unreviewed to blind or low-vision users, content moderation, safety or compliance labelling, surveillance descriptions of people — without a human comparing the caption with the image, and a locally measured CIDEr/BLEU on the deployment's own reference-captioned images.

#### Factors

###### Groups

This pipeline is human-centric wherever the image contains people: COCO captions describe people by apparent gender, age, activity and dress ("a man", "a young girl", "an old woman"), and the model reproduces those conventions — including the annotators' guesses — for anyone it depicts, with no signal that it is guessing. Published audits of COCO document gender-imbalanced activity associations and a skew towards Western, adult, lighter-skinned subjects; neither the upstream authors nor this repository audited caption accuracy or caption *content* (which attributes are mentioned, which words are chosen) by the depicted person's gender, age, skin tone, disability, dress or setting, nor the pretraining web corpora for stereotyped image–text pairs. Non-human groups whose accuracy is unknown, not known to be equal: non-photographic images (the tutorial's cartoons; the model miscounted their fruit), non-Western scenes, objects, food and clothing, low-light and low-resolution captures, and images whose salient content is text. An operator whose images contain people is responsible for a fairness audit on their own image set, stratified by depicted group, before relying on the output — and for deciding whether descriptions of people are surfaced at all (see §Use cases).

###### Instrumentation

The upstream pretraining "instrument" is web image–text pairs at whatever resolution and quality the source sites served, filtered by BLIP's own captioner/filter, and the fine-tuning instrument is COCO photography (consumer cameras, Flickr uploads) with crowd-written captions. Inference images arrive from whatever produced them — a phone camera, a screenshot, a render, a scanner — and resolution, exposure, colour balance, compression and aspect ratio all change the visual evidence; the fixed 384×384 resize discards aspect ratio and detail regardless of the source, so a 4096×4096 input carries no more information than a 384×384 one. The pipeline validates type, size and prefix length only; it cannot detect an unusual capture or a non-photographic image. The synthetic tutorial scenes (flat Pillow shapes, no texture, no lighting) are themselves a rendering instrument unlike any COCO photograph, which is why the smoke run's miscount is recorded rather than treated as an anomaly.

###### Environment

Operating environment: Python 3.12 with `torch==2.14.0`, `torchvision==0.29.0`, `torchaudio==2.11.0`, `transformers==4.57.6`, `safetensors==0.8.0` (installed as a pin; unused by this checkpoint), `numpy==2.5.3`, `pillow==11.3.0`, float32 on CPU; CUDA is used automatically when visible (float32) but was not exercised for this card. Measured on the reference machine with the GPU hidden (`CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=-1`) and the Hub offline (`HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1`): `verify_snapshot` on the 8-file, 990 MB snapshot (included in the load); load 3.9 s with exactly two `torch.load(weights_only=True)` calls observed; three drawn scenes, unconditional and with two prefixes, 0.18–0.54 s per caption (2–11 tokens; first call slower); a 4096×4096 blank image 0.49 s — cost is one 576-patch encoder pass plus a few decoder steps per image, roughly independent of the input resolution after the resize. Data environment: the model assumes the image is an everyday photograph and, when a prefix is given, that the prefix is the start of a COCO-style sentence; the synthetic tutorial scenes violate the first assumption on purpose (flat cartoons), which is where the recorded miscount comes from. Documents, diagrams, artwork, empty or corrupted images violate it to degrees this repository did not measure, and the pipeline reports no signal when they do.

#### Metrics

###### Performance Measures

The pipeline reports no quality measure. The caption is generated text with no score, probability or correctness signal; `new_tokens` and `truncated` describe the generation, not its quality. Caption quality is conventionally measured with reference-based corpus metrics — CIDEr, BLEU-4, METEOR, SPICE — which need several human-written references per image and a corpus; none is implemented here, and COCO Captions is not bundled. The repository ships two lighter helpers because they are what a caller can compute on their own labelled sample: `unigram_f1(prediction, references)` — bag-of-words F1 after normalisation (lower-cased, punctuation removed, whitespace collapsed) against the best-matching reference, multiset overlap — and `keyword_hits(caption, keywords)`, whole-token presence of caller-chosen keywords. Both are plumbing checks, stated as such. The public `evaluation_report(results, references=None)` stage returns the report in machine-readable form: the mean `unigram_f1` plus one per-image entry (prediction, references, score) with the verdict `sample-sanity` when references are supplied, or the verdict `not-measurable` naming the labelled set that would be required when they are not — which is the tutorial's default path, by design. The upstream paper's COCO Captions CIDEr (about 130 for its ViT-B models with beam search, upstream-reported; which pretraining variant the hosted checkpoint corresponds to is not stated on the upstream card) is not reproduced or claimed by this pipeline.

###### Decision thresholds

No score threshold exists in the model: it generates tokens until end-of-sequence or the budget and nothing is filtered or abstained. The decision parameters are the **token budget** `max_new_tokens`, default `DEFAULT_MAX_NEW_TOKENS = 30` (a sentence-sized budget chosen by this repository; the checkpoint's own `text_config.max_length` is 20 and the smoke captions needed 2–11 tokens including end-of-sequence) with ceiling `MAX_NEW_TOKENS = 64`, and the optional **prefix**, which the model continues verbatim and which therefore shapes the caption (`a drawing of` yielded `a drawing of a house and tree`; no prefix yielded `a red house with a tree and a ball`). A budget that is too small is reported, not hidden: `truncated` is true whenever `new_tokens` reaches it, and the caller should raise the budget and rerun. Decoding is greedy (`do_sample=False`, `num_beams=1`) with no temperature, beams or repetition penalty — a reproducibility choice that departs from the upstream beam-search evaluation setting. A deployment owns choosing the budget and prefix and deciding how a caption is verified against the image before it is used.

###### Approaches to uncertainty and variability

This repository reports no central metric value and therefore no dispersion: the smoke run records timings, token counts and the captions on three drawn scenes, not quality. Run-to-run variability comes only from floating-point kernel selection across CPU builds and accelerators; there is no sampling and no seed to set, so a fixed input on fixed hardware is repeatable, but because decoding is autoregressive a single differing token changes the rest of the sentence, and CPU and CUDA outputs need not match; the drawn scenes use no text rendering, so their bytes do not depend on the Pillow build. On the drawn scenes the model produced `a red house with a tree and a ball`, `a beach scene with a sail and a sun` and `a red and yellow apple on a white background` (the orange became an apple) — three observations on flat cartoons, not an estimate; on a blank white image it produced `a white and black striped rug with a white border` and on uniform noise `a very colorful and very colorful tv screen`, which is what an uncalibrated generator with no abstention looks like. A caller who needs a quality estimate must supply several reference captions per image and compute CIDEr/BLEU over a corpus or bootstrap resamples themselves; a caller who needs a confidence per caption has none from this model.

#### Ethical considerations and biases

No external ethics board, red-team, or population-specific clearance reviewed this repository or, to our knowledge, the upstream checkpoint; nothing below should be read as implying one.

###### Data

The upstream paper describes pretraining on 129M image–text pairs from COCO, Visual Genome, Conceptual Captions 3M and 12M, SBU Captions and a 115M-image LAION subset — web-scraped pairs whose images and captions can include real, identifiable people, copyrighted photographs and stereotyped or offensive text, filtered only by BLIP's own learned filter — and fine-tuning on COCO Captions, whose images are Flickr photographs of people and everyday scenes with crowd-written descriptions; personal data in the training corpora is therefore present by construction. Neither was audited here. This repository distributes code, tests, and documentation; it does not distribute the 989,820,849-byte `pytorch_model.bin` (staged locally under `weights/blip-image-captioning-base/` and git-ignored) nor the 990,275,136-byte `tf_model.h5` (never fetched by this package), and it ships no sample photographs — the tutorial scenes are drawn in code. The operator must audit the images they submit for personal, proprietary, or otherwise restricted content; the pipeline performs no such check and will describe a person's appearance as readily as a landscape.

###### Human Life

This pipeline is not intended for decisions in health, safety, criminal justice, employment, credit, or housing, and it has not been validated or certified for any of them by this repository, the upstream authors, or any regulator. Foreseeable but unintended sensitive uses — publishing captions as alt-text to blind or low-vision users where a wrong description misleads, describing people in surveillance or bodycam footage, labelling user-uploaded images for moderation or safety, describing medical or hazard imagery — would be admissible only with human review of every caption against the image (the model invents content when there is none and gives no signal), a locally measured reference-based score on the deployment's own captioned images stratified by depicted group, a documented budget and prefix policy with `truncated` handling, an explicit policy on whether people are described at all, and whatever regulatory clearance the domain requires.

###### Mitigations

- **Supply-chain integrity:** `MODEL_REVISION` is a 40-hex commit; `stage_missing_files` refuses a manifest whose `modelId`/`revision` differ from the package constants and fetches only manifest-listed files at that revision when `allow_download=True`; `verify_snapshot` then checks all 8 listed files' byte sizes and SHA-256 before any load — **including the pickle checkpoint, so a modified `pytorch_model.bin` never reaches a deserialiser**; `from_pretrained` loads only from the verified directory with `local_files_only=True`, always passes `trust_remote_code=False`, `use_safetensors=False` (naming the format) and `weights_only=True` (restricted unpickler: tensors, primitives and containers only), and the smoke run loaded and captioned with `HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1` while observing exactly two `torch.load(weights_only=True)` calls. The upstream `tf_model.h5` is neither listed nor loaded. A test flips one hex digit of a manifest digest and asserts the loader refuses; another asserts a foreign manifest is refused; the import-boundary tests assert that a missing or tampered snapshot is refused before `torch` or `transformers` is imported; another asserts the manifest lists the pickle and not the TensorFlow file.
- **Input integrity:** the public `validate_inputs(images, *, prefix, max_new_tokens)` stage applies exactly the checks `caption` applies (both route through one shared private checker) and returns an input manifest recording the schema, the ceilings, the observed inputs, the checked prefix, the budget and the verdict; `validate_image` rejects non-PIL inputs and sides outside 16–4096 px; non-string, empty or over-long prefixes and out-of-range or boolean budgets are rejected; `caption` raises on a malformed runner result; `evaluation_report` rejects mismatched or empty reference lists; `unigram_f1` rejects an empty reference set.
- **Reproducibility:** exact `==` pins in `pyproject.toml`; greedy single-beam decoding with no sampling; every result carries `model_id`, `model_revision`, the checked prefix, the budget, `new_tokens`, `truncated`, the device and the dtype.
- **Refusals:** no batching, no download without the explicit flag, no Hub access at inference time, no sampling, no unrestricted pickle deserialisation, no attempt to guess whether the image has anything to describe, and no filtering of caption content — that policy is the operator's to implement around the pipeline.
- No statistical mitigation (class balancing, subsampling) applies: no training happens in this repository.

###### Risks and harms

- **Invented content:** the model has no abstention — a blank image yielded a striped rug and noise a colourful TV screen in the smoke run — so an empty, corrupted or unusual image produces a fluent, plausible caption with no signal; downstream consumers that trust the string (alt-text pipelines, search indexes, LLM pipelines) inherit the error silently.
- **Hallucinated attributes:** colours, backgrounds, counts and objects that are not there (`yellow apple`, `white background`, a second apple) are stated with the same fluency as correct ones; only reference-based evaluation on the deployment's images shows the rate, and the tutorial measures none.
- **Descriptions of people:** apparent gender, age and activity are asserted from appearance in COCO's conventions; misgendering, age errors and stereotyped activity associations are foreseeable and can cause direct harm when surfaced.
- **Prefix steering:** a conditional caption is partly the caller's sentence; a leading prefix (`a photo of a criminal`) makes the model complete it.
- **Automation bias:** a grammatical one-sentence caption invites trust that generated text has not earned.
- **Truncation:** a caption longer than the budget is cut (reported via `truncated`); a caller who ignores the flag ships a partial sentence.
- **Pickle checkpoint:** the executed weight file is a pickle; the digest check and `weights_only=True` are the mitigation, and a caller who bypasses `from_pretrained` and loads the file another way forfeits both.
- **Bias amplification:** any image population COCO under-represents (non-Western scenes, non-photographic images, minority groups) is reproduced as uneven caption quality and word choice, undetected because no per-group evaluation exists.
- **Resource use:** a 990 MB model and ~0.2–0.5 s per image on the reference CPU; an image-heavy workload scales linearly, and the CUDA path was not measured.

###### Use cases

Prohibited even where the model would work: captioning images of people in order to infer or record protected characteristics (race, ethnicity, religion, health, disability, sexual orientation), to identify, track, profile or surveil individuals, or to make or support decisions in employment, housing, credit, insurance, education, healthcare access, law enforcement or immigration; processing images the operator has no right to process, including intimate imagery and licence-restricted material; publishing captions as verified descriptions, evidence or alt-text without human review; steering the model with prefixes to produce defamatory, sexual or demeaning descriptions of real people; and any use that violates the upstream BSD-3-Clause licence terms (including use of the Salesforce name for endorsement), the DIMER deployment terms, or the consent and data-protection obligations attached to the images processed. Autonomous high-consequence actions triggered by unreviewed captions are prohibited by the intended-use contract above.

## Input/output contract

- `BlipCaptioningPipeline.from_pretrained(device=None, weights_dir=None, allow_download=False)` — stages missing manifest files (only with `allow_download=True`), verifies digests, loads; `device` defaults to `cuda:0` when visible, else `cpu`; float32 on both.
- `caption(image, *, prefix=None, max_new_tokens=30) -> dict` with keys `caption` (stripped decoded text; the prefix is echoed first when given), `prefix` (whitespace-collapsed or `None`), `image_size`, `new_tokens` (tokens generated after the prefix, end-of-sequence included), `truncated`, `generation` (`max_new_tokens`, `do_sample` false, `decoding` greedy), `device`, `dtype`, `source`, `model_id`, `model_revision`.
- `normalize_caption(text) -> str`; `caption_tokens(text) -> list[str]`; `keyword_hits(caption, keywords) -> dict[str, bool]`; `unigram_f1(prediction, references) -> float`.
- Ceilings and constants: `MIN_IMAGE_SIDE = 16`, `MAX_IMAGE_SIDE = 4096`, `MAX_PREFIX_CHARS = 128`, `MAX_NEW_TOKENS = 64`, `DEFAULT_MAX_NEW_TOKENS = 30`, `DECODING = "greedy"`, `IMAGE_SIZE = 384`, `WEIGHT_FILE = "pytorch_model.bin"`, `HOSTED_TF_WEIGHT_FILE = "tf_model.h5"`, `INPUT_SCHEMA`.
- `validate_inputs(images, *, prefix, max_new_tokens, names) -> dict`; `evaluation_report(results, references=None, *, sample_kind) -> dict` where `references` holds one sequence of reference captions per result; `verify_snapshot(path=None) -> dict`; `stage_missing_files(path=None, *, allow_download=False, downloader=None) -> list[str]`.

## Runtime

- Pins: `torch==2.14.0`, `torchvision==0.29.0`, `torchaudio==2.11.0`, `transformers==4.57.6`, `safetensors==0.8.0`, `numpy==2.5.3`, `pillow==11.3.0`, `huggingface-hub==0.36.2`; Python 3.12.
- Precision: float32; preprocessing resizes the image to 384×384 (aspect ratio not preserved) and normalises with CLIP mean/std (`BlipImageProcessor`, snapshot defaults); an optional prefix is tokenised by the snapshot's BERT tokenizer; greedy single-beam decoding.
- Measured 2026-09-14 in the Windows venv (`torch 2.14.0+cu130`) with `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=-1` and `HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1`, device `cpu`: load 3.9 s (verify included; two `torch.load(weights_only=True)` calls on `pytorch_model.bin` observed); `caption` on three synthetic cartoon scenes drawn with Pillow — house scene (640×480): no prefix `a red house with a tree and a ball` (10 tokens, 0.54 s first call), `a photography of` → `a photography of a red house in a green field` (8 tokens, 0.26 s), `a drawing of` → `a drawing of a house and tree` (0.22 s); beach scene (640×480): `a beach scene with a sail and a sun`, `a photography of a beach scene`, `a drawing of a beach with a sail and a sun`; fruit scene (480×480, an apple and an orange): `a red and yellow apple on a white background`, `a photography of two apples`, `a drawing of two apples on a white background`; 0.18–0.28 s each after the first; blank 384×384 and 4096×4096 white images → `a white and black striped rug with a white border` (11 tokens; 0.49 s at 4096²); uniform noise → `a very colorful and very colorful tv screen`; `max_new_tokens=3` → `a red house`, `truncated` true; `keyword_hits` on the house caption: house/tree/red present, sun absent.
- Tutorial execution: `tutorials/blip_captioning_colab.ipynb` ran top-to-bottom in a fresh local kernel (all 8 code cells, 101.8 s including the 990 MB staging of the pickle checkpoint, same three captions as the smoke run, `tf_model.h5` not fetched); recorded in `docs/release-verification.md` as pre-flight, not supported-runtime evidence.
- Tests: `pytest -q -o addopts= tests` — offline, no weights required; `ruff check src tests tools` clean.
- Not executed: CUDA path, photographs (only drawn scenes, blank images and noise), beam search (the upstream evaluation setting), any reference-based metric, the `tf_model.h5` artifact (never loaded; equivalence to the pickle not verified), images of people.
